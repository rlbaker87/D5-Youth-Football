1st & 2nd Grade Division

Flag Football Rules

A parent & coach guide to how the game works — no football experience required.

1st & 10

How the Game Works

The short version, before we break down every rule:

7 vs. 7. Seven offensive players against seven defenders, and every team must use a center.

Running clock, ~50 minutes. The clock keeps moving between plays, like a real game clock — it doesn't stop and start the way it might in other youth sports.

One coach on the field. Only one coach per team is allowed on the field during games.

Every play is a forward pass. There are no hand-offs or pitches in this division — the quarterback throws to a receiver on every play.

Two timeouts per team. The clock does not stop for timeouts.

Touches, not tackles. Defenders stop the ball carrier with a one-hand touch instead of a tackle.

2nd & 10

The Field & How a Drive Works

Every possession starts at the 40-yard line. From there, the offense drives toward the goal line, picking up new first downs along the way.

Each possession starts at the 40 · crossing every 10-yard line earns a new first down · PATs snap from the 3-yard line

First downs: the offense earns a new first down every time it crosses a 10-yard line. After that, the ball is placed at the closest 10-yard line for the next set of downs.

Downs & Possession

Downs & Possession

First downs — cross a 10-yard line, get a fresh set of downs. The ball resets to the closest 10-yard line.

Turnover on downs — if the offense doesn't reach the next 10-yard line by 4th down, the ball goes to the other team.

Sacks — there's no rushing the quarterback in this division. A "sack" only happens if the QB holds the ball more than 5 seconds. It costs a loss of down, with the ball placed where the QB was when time ran out (unless that spot is behind the offense's own 40-yard line, in which case there's no yardage loss — just the down).

The 5-Second Clock

The 5-Second Clock

There's no pass rush in this division — defenders can't rush the quarterback. Instead, a stopwatch keeps things moving.

The clock starts the moment the ball is snapped. An official counts to 5 with a stopwatch.

If the QB hasn't thrown by then, the official raises a hand — the play still finishes (so everyone gets the rep), but it results in a loss of down at the spot of the QB.

Think of it as a delay-of-game penalty, not a real sack — nobody is allowed to rush the passer.

Touching, Fumbles & Interceptions

Touching, Fumbles & Interceptions

One-hand touch ends the play — instead of a tackle, a defender just needs one hand on the ball carrier.

Fumbles are dead the moment the ball hits the ground. There's no scramble for it — the ball is simply placed where it was fumbled, and no blocking is allowed on the play.

Interceptions are dead on the spot — the defense can't return them. An interception is worth 3 points on its own and then the offense that threw it gets the ball back.
